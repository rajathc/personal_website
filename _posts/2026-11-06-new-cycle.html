After almost 10 years, I have a new cycle. Back in 2017, I wanted a cycle to commute to classes and the labs at IIT Bombay, so I got my Montra Blues 1.1. It has served me really well, with over 15k km of riding: all across Mumbai, then daily 20-30 km workout rides in Mandya during the pandemic, then the cycle path in Hyderabad and finally around Lalbagh in Bangalore. It has been with me through a lot, and I have so many great memories with it. But I think it's time to retire it. It has an aluminium frame, and its biggest drawback was the front suspension.

{% comment %}PHOTO (optional): the Montra, ideally an old one from Mumbai or IIT Bombay.{% endcomment %}

A few years ago, I'd even managed to replace the freewheel and a bunch of other parts, but it was turning into a money pit. So this is a farewell, for now. I'll see if I can make it a project bike later. In the meantime, I have a new cycle to introduce.

Rajath goes Roadie

It was a long-time dream to own a drop bar. On those long rides in Mumbai, 2017 Rajath would look enviously at the people on their Trek and Specialized road bikes. I just found them cool. I considered getting a road bike multiple times, but brushed the idea aside each time, as I didn't want a bike I couldn't use for commuting or my fun rides.

Much later, I started looking at Surly bikes. I envied them too, and even checked them out at Bums on the Saddle during the Preamble launch in India, but the price made them feel out of reach. By then, though, I had my heart set on a chromoly steel bike, so I kept looking for a cheaper option. Finally, I found a State Bicycle Co. 4130 Road for sale, with its unique downtube shifter, and decided to pull the trigger.

{% comment %}PHOTO (optional): close-up of the downtube shifter.{% endcomment %}

One thing to note is that I'm a modern classic guy. Even my motorbike is an Interceptor 650, which has all the perks of a new bike but looks like an old one. The State 4130 Road is the bicycle version of that. I just love looking at its silver accents and the colourway, which is called Americana.

{% comment %}PHOTO (optional): the silver parts and Americana paint, or the bike parked next to the Interceptor.{% endcomment %}

Funnily enough, the kind of bike I once thought I couldn't commute on is now my commuter. I've been riding it to the gym and to Lulu.

The drop bar did take some getting used to. Visibility isn't great even on the hoods, and the aggressive riding position doesn't help. But I've gotten used to it now, and I'm sure it'll feel even more natural in a month.

Still, my first impression was that this thing goes like a rocket. Coming from the Montra's suspension fork, the difference is huge. With the rigid fork and chromoly steel frame, all the power I put down on the pedals goes straight into moving the bike forward. The narrow handlebar makes it nimble too. I can't wait to take it on a longer ride once I've got it dialled in.

Stay tuned for more bike adventures. I'm working on a challenge of sorts, and I have more things to write about. For now, I just wanted to show off my cool new-for-me bike.
